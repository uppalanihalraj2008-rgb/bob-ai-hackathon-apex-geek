# Problem Statement

## Background

[Disaster Victim Identification (DVI) is the forensic process used to identify
deceased individuals after mass-fatality events such as floods, earthquakes,
building collapses, and accidents.

The process compares:

- **Ante-mortem (AM) data** — information about missing persons, including
  physical characteristics, dental records, photographs, and distinguishing
  marks.
- **Post-mortem (PM) data** — information collected from recovered remains,
  including physical characteristics, photographs, dental findings, and
  fingerprints where available.

Manually comparing large numbers of AM and PM records can be time-consuming.
This project provides an explainable, human-in-the-loop system that combines
structured evidence with facial and fingerprint comparison to prioritize
potential matches for forensic investigators.]

## The Problem

[During mass-fatality events, large numbers of ante-mortem (AM) and
post-mortem (PM) records may need to be compared within a short period.
Manual cross-referencing of these records is time-consuming and can make
it difficult for investigators to identify promising matches quickly.

The challenge is to compare multiple partially reliable signals such as
physical characteristics, distinguishing marks, dental information,
photographs, and available biometric evidence while keeping the process
explainable.

A system is therefore needed to prioritize potential AM-PM matches,
provide clear supporting evidence and scores, and maintain human
oversight so that final identification remains the responsibility of
qualified forensic investigators.]

## Who is Affected

[
- **Families:** Face uncertainty while waiting for the identification of
  missing or deceased relatives.
- **Forensic Investigators:** Need to compare large amounts of AM and PM
  information efficiently and consistently.
- **Disaster Response Teams:** Handle large volumes of records during
  mass-fatality incidents.
- **Medical and Forensic Institutions:** Manage sensitive identification
  records and coordinate evidence across cases.
- **Authorities:** Require reliable, explainable information to support the
  formal identification process.]

## Why It Matters

[Fast and reliable identification helps reduce uncertainty for families and
supports forensic teams during mass-fatality investigations. An explainable
reconciliation system can reduce the time spent manually comparing records,
prioritize promising candidates, and provide investigators with clear evidence
to support further forensic review.

The system is designed to assist qualified investigators while keeping the
final identification decision under human oversight.]

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

[
- **Manual comparison:** Investigators may need to compare large numbers of
  AM and PM records manually, which is slow and difficult to scale.
- **Fragmented information:** Relevant evidence such as physical descriptions,
  dental records, photographs, and biometric information may exist across
  different records or systems.
- **Limited explainability:** Some automated approaches may provide a result
  without clearly showing the evidence behind a potential match.
- **Scalability:** Traditional workflows become increasingly difficult to
  manage as the number of missing persons and recovered individuals grows.
- **Human oversight:** Identification requires forensic expertise and review,
  so automated systems should assist investigators rather than make the final
  identification decision.]
